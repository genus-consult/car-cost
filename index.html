How the calculation works

A transparent financial model, not a black-box score.

1. Convert the risk-free rate to monthly
monthly rate = (1 + annual rate)^(1/12) − 1
2. Calculate present value of cash flows
PV cost = upfront purchases + PV(monthly bills) + PV(insurance renewals) + PV(battery EMIs) + PV(loan settlement) − PV(resale)
3. Express as an equivalent monthly cost
economic ₹/month = PV cost ÷ Σ[1/(1 + monthly rate)^m], for m = 1 to months owned
We assume cash expenses occur at the end of each month, except vehicle and charger purchase at the start; insurance renewals occur at months 12, 24, etc. Resale and any outstanding battery loan settlement occur at the end of ownership.
4. Battery-loan mechanics
battery EMI = P × r / [1 − (1+r)^(-n)] (r = annual loan rate / 12)

The EMI is paid only while the loan is active. If the ownership period ends before the loan, the outstanding balance is settled at sale and added to cost. If it ends afterward, EMI payments stop once the loan is repaid.

Assumptions & source notes
  • Both purchase prices are hypothetical ₹12 lakh on-road figures. The EV price excludes the separately financed battery, while EV resale includes the battery.
  • Year 1 insurance is inside each purchase price. ₹15,000 renewal is paid for Years 2 onward. No insurance is charged twice.
  • 365 days/year; monthly running is the annual distance divided by 12. Consumption at the wall includes charging losses.
  • Driver cost is identical for both, but included in the full cost of ownership.
  • Optional annual cost escalation is initially 0%; all values are forecasts and can be updated after receiving quotations.
  • Kia's Syros EV BaaS terms describe a dual-loan product. The advertised ₹3.30/km is an illustrative EMI equivalent at 2,200 km/month, not a confirmed metered subscription. Our ₹5.5 lakh at 9.5% for 60 months is an assumed example, not Kia's guaranteed pricing.
