# Research findings, caveats and source register

**Reviewed:** 7 November 2026. **Purpose:** retain what was researched, what it actually establishes, and what must be confirmed before buying in Kolkata. This is an independent family model, not an official Kia offer or legal/insurance advice.

## Evidence labels used in this project

- **Confirmed source statement:** supported by a linked official manufacturer, insurer, regulator or utility publication, as of the stated review date.
- **Model assumption:** a number selected for a meaningful comparison, **not** an actual offer or expected market price.
- **Unverified / pending:** needs a real contract, policy or written quotation.
- **Superseded:** an earlier working assumption that should **not** be interpreted as the current site default.

## Kia Syros EV BaaS: what we learned

**Primary source:** [Kia Syros EV terms and conditions](https://www.kia.com/in/utility/Syros-EV-t-c.html); also [Kia Syros EV showroom](https://www.kia.com/in/our-vehicles/syros-ev/showroom.html). Checked on 7 November 2026.

| Topic | Established from Kia's publication | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Product structure | Kia calls Battery-as-a-Service (**BaaS**) a **dual-loan finance product**, financing the battery and chassis separately. | This is closer to **battery financing** than to pure pay-per-kilometre battery rental. |
| Advertised starting BaaS price | ₹7.99 lakh **ex-showroom** for the vehicle under the offer. | Excludes government/statutory charges, road tax, insurance, TCS and any applicable extras. **Not** a Kolkata ₹8L on-road quotation. |
| Advertised battery pricing | ₹3.30/km **illustrative equivalent** at assumed **2,200 km/month** on the standard-range offering. | 3.30 × 2,200 = **₹7,260/month implied illustrative EMI**. It does **not** confirm a charge of 3.30 × *actual* kilometres. |
| Financing terms | Subject to lender/NBFC credit appraisal; actual loan amount, interest rate, tenure and lender terms determine the final offer. | Neither 60 months nor the ₹7,260 amount is guaranteed. |
| Actual monthly kilometres | No actual-odometer billing formula was established in the cited official terms. | A fixed EMI regardless of 0/1,200/3,000 km is the **working interpretation** of a normal loan, but get written confirmation of any usage cap, excess-km charge or other conditions. |
| EV charger | Kia says charger is **not standard fitment** and is separately purchasable. | Our ₹75,000 complete home installation estimate is independent of Kia pricing. |
| Battery warranty | Kia describes a 15-year/unlimited-km high-voltage battery warranty for the **first private owner**; second owner's protection is **8 years/160,000 km** (whichever earlier), subject to terms. | The 'lifetime' headline does **not** mean identical coverage on resale or unrestricted commercial use. |
| Assured buyback | Kia advertises a 3-year buyback programme with promotional conditions. | **Do not substitute** this programme for a guaranteed **five-year** ₹5L resale quotation. |

**Critical contrast from the discussion:** A reply from another person supplied a *sample* battery loan of **₹5.5L at 9.5% for 60 months**, giving about **₹11,551 EMI/month**. It also described typical 3–5 year tenures, transfer possibilities, battery ownership and insurance. These are **not verified Kia contract terms**. We deliberately adopted only the numbers as a **scenario** so the model could proceed.

**Do not combine:** the advertised **₹7,260** EMI equivalent with a presumed five-year loan on **₹5.5L at 9.5%**; they cannot all hold for the same standard amortising loan. A five-year ₹5.5L loan at 9.5% requires roughly ₹11,551/month. Using ₹7,260 with that same five-year loan would understate cost or omit remaining principal.

**Sale at five years:** Current model assumes gross EV resale **₹5L including battery**. If the battery loan is not fully paid by month 60, the remaining balance is settled at sale. Actual loan novation/transfer and foreclosure terms are **unknown**, and may affect what can be transferred to a buyer. Warranty transfer is also relevant to resale.

### Questions to get answered *in writing* by dealer / financing partner

1. Which precise variant/battery is quoted? What is **Kolkata on-road cash price for the vehicle excluding separately financed battery**, including first-year insurance and statutory charges?
2. What is **battery loan principal**, bank/NBFC name, down payment, processing charge, APR, repayment method, **term** and **actual fixed EMI**? Obtain an amortisation schedule.
3. Is EMI independent of kilometres? Is there any usage cap, minimum-km obligation, excess-km billing or battery-usage monitoring fee?
4. Who is legally the battery owner during and after financing? Which hypothecation, collateral, or vehicle RC documents apply?
5. Can car and battery be sold freely after five years? What is the contractual **foreclosure fee**, balance statement, NOC process, and loan-transfer policy?
6. Does comprehensive insurance cover the battery for theft, flooding and collision, and what are exclusions or special add-ons? Who bears deductibles?
7. What battery warranty, degradation standards and transfer rights will a **second owner** have? Does any buyback guarantee apply at **five** years?
8. Does the charger/wiring require separate equipment, permissions, load upgrade or utility meter? What total installed amount will be paid?

## Indian car insurance: fundamentals and simplification

Sources:
- [Zurich Kotak: minimum insurance period for a new car, updated 5 Nov 2026](https://www.zurichkotak.com/knowledge-center/car-insurance/minimum-insurance-period-new-car-india)
- [IFFCO Tokio: IRDAI guidance on bundled new-car insurance](https://www.iffcotokio.co.in/about-us/irdai-important-circulars/withdrawal-of-package-cover-for-new-private-cars-and-new-two-wheelers)

**Third-party (TP):** insurance against certain legal liability for harm to other people/property. **Mandatory for driving on public roads**, but does not repair the insured owner's vehicle. **Own-damage (OD):** protection for the owner's vehicle against covered damage/theft; generally **optional**, subject to policy conditions. Comprehensive or bundled policies commonly combine TP and OD. Owner-driver personal accident cover has separate rules/exemptions; confirm them when buying, especially if a paid driver will also use the vehicle.

As of the insurer's 5 November 2026 guidance, **three-year upfront TP** remained the standard for new private cars, with **one-year OD** commonly bundled; there had also been a **2026 direction to extend new-car TP duration to four years**, requiring a future implementing direction. **Insurance rules are date-sensitive**—verify the applicable term when purchasing rather than freezing this into the calculator.

**Our decision for simplicity:** the first year's bundled insurance cost is within upfront assumed on-road price. Use **₹15,000/year for Years 2–5 for each car**, although the *actual* year-by-year OD/TP renewal mix, taxes, no-claim bonus, IDV, add-ons, EV battery cover and any future law changes will differ. At sale, leftover prepaid insurance is **not automatically cash payable on top of the used-car selling price**; transfer/refund procedures and negotiation vary. The plan to make a dedicated insurance quote/details module in the future remains **unimplemented**.

## Electricity and home charging

Sources:
- [CESC official tariff and monthly adjustments](https://www.cesc.co.in/tariff)
- [WBERC tariff order register for CESC](https://wberc.gov.in/tariff-related-order?field_tariff_petition_matter_value=Tariff+Order&field_utility_value=CESC+Ltd)

CESC domestic tariffs use **slabs**, and additional consumption may change marginal energy charges; fuel/power-purchase adjustments, duty, fixed fees, meter category and any special EV/time-of-day plan may matter. The discussion referenced a high domestic energy slab of approximately **₹9.21/kWh as a base rate** under a published tariff, **not** a complete all-in incremental bill rate.

**Model choice:** use **₹11/kWh** as a deliberately conservative, *editable*, all-in **incremental cost of EV charging**, instead of attempting to replicate a household's bill. Default **0.20 kWh/km at the wall already includes charging inefficiency**. Do not apply an extra charging-loss percentage to the same energy again. Default **₹75,000 upfront** includes charger, installation and wiring as a planning allowance. These are not quotations. User can change the tariff to **₹6.80/kWh**, which was shown to reproduce a family screenshot with EV full monthly cost **₹42,279**; that does **not** establish ₹6.80 as the tariff actually payable.

Before purchase, compare **household bill before/after charging**, electricity category, extra load approval, separate EV meter possibility, wiring distance and electrician's quote. If a separate meter/connection is used, account for any incremental fixed charges or deposit; the current flat ₹/kWh estimate doesn't model those separately.

## Petrol, maintenance, driving and resale caveats

- The petrol **₹114/L** is an **assumed fixed price**, not a guaranteed multi-year Kolkata quote.
- Petrol **10 km/L** and EV **0.20 wall kWh/km** are conservative practical efficiency assumptions, **not validated tests** for a chosen variant under Kolkata traffic/weather.
- Driving **40 km/day × 365 days** assumes travel every day; real weekly patterns and occasional highway trips can differ.
- Routine maintenance: ₹10k petrol / ₹5k EV per year are placeholders. Neither car model includes unusual repairs, tyres, accidents, parking, tolls, accessories, public DC charging, financing fees, replacement equipment, battery degradation costs or full insurance claim excess.
- **₹5L resale** is a comparable hypothetical gross value after five years and ~73,000 km, **not** a used-car forecast. EV second-owner battery warranty and finance clearance may change achievable price.
- Driver pay ₹18k/month is included in *both* choices, so it affects their absolute costs but usually not which is cheaper. No pay growth is assumed.

## Discount rate caveat

The user chose **6% effective annual opportunity cost** and **0% running-cost escalation**. The 6% is not a claim that a government bond or deposit delivers a guaranteed 6% net of tax. Use **0%** to get a simple net nominal five-year expense divided by months; the battery EMI still includes **9.5% loan interest**. See [calculation methodology](calculation-method.md).

## Source discipline and periodic review

The above official links were reviewed on **7 November 2026**. Rates, laws, dealer offers and warranty details **can change**. In this project:
1. Prefer **written dealer/lender quotations** and dated official terms over a conversational summary or online forum.
2. Preserve units (₹/month, ₹/year, ₹/km, ₹/kWh, km/year) and whether an amount is **ex-showroom / on-road / principal / EMI / effective monthly economic cost**.
3. Record **exact variant, date, coverage and location** for every future quotation.
4. Separate **evidence**, **model assumptions**, and **contractual terms** in both docs and UI.
5. Never invent financing terms to make a marketing rate match a preferred period; test the arithmetic.


## 10 November 2026 — hybrid template caveats

A **self-charging strong hybrid** has both an internal combustion engine and an electric drive motor but normally gets its replenishment energy from petrol and regenerative braking, **not** paid external grid electricity. For the default hybrid comparison use a single assumed combined real-world **18 km/L**; do **not** separately credit 'free electric kilometres'. The assumed 18 km/L is *not* a verified figure for a specific make, model or Kolkata test drive.

Plug-in hybrids have an externally charged battery and can incur electricity bills. For them, the calculator accepts an estimated fraction of driving km powered by grid electricity and allocates the rest to petrol-mode mileage. Actual real-world consumption depends strongly on charging frequency, trip length and ambient conditions. This is a simplifying scenario input, not an official homologation or engineering model.

The hybrid price (₹12L), insurance (₹15k/year), maintenance (₹10k/year), resale (₹5L) and ownership life (5 years) are still placeholders until real quotations and test drives arrive. A hybrid's battery warranty, servicing requirements and five-year resale need variant-specific evidence. See [test-drive notes](decision-process.md).
